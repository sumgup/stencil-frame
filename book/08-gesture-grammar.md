# Gesture Grammar

> Gesture is jurisdiction.
>
> Every touch declares the right to act upon something.

---

# Purpose

Gesture Grammar defines who is permitted to leave a mark.

It is not a catalogue of movements.

It is a theory of authority.

If Motion Grammar governs how judgment unfolds through time, Gesture Grammar governs who has earned the right to perform that judgment.

Every gesture changes standing.

Every touch carries jurisdiction.

---

# Governing Question

**Who has earned the right to make this mark?**

---

# First Principles

A gesture is not movement.

A gesture is movement backed by authority.

The same physical action can carry different meaning depending on who performs it.

Gesture therefore concerns permission rather than mechanics.

Nothing is touched simply because it can be.

Everything touched becomes a claim.

---

# Jurisdiction

The system contains four territories.

## Centre

The founder's territory.

Only the founder may author here.

Stencil never occupies the centre.

---

## Margin

Stencil's territory.

Questions.

Objections.

Alternatives.

Annotations.

Stencil always speaks beside.

Never instead.

---

## Surface

Shared.

Both founder and Stencil may inspect.

Neither owns it.

---

## Archive

Read-only.

Neither actor edits.

Both actors cite.

---

# Laws

## Law 1 — Contact

Contact must be earned.

No gesture begins in motion.

Every gesture begins with permission.

---

## Law 2 — One Gesture

One gesture.

One intention.

Compound gestures conceal responsibility.

A correction remains a correction.

An annotation remains an annotation.

Never combine acts into one flourish.

---

## Law 3 — Withdrawal

The gesture is incomplete until the hand leaves.

Withdrawal grants standing.

A mark without withdrawal remains provisional.

The lift matters as much as the stroke.

---

## Law 4 — Margin

Stencil never writes over the founder.

Stencil writes beside.

Distance preserves authorship.

---

## Law 5 — Refusal

No gesture may compel agreement.

Every mark remains contestable.

Every suggestion may be refused.

Refusal carries equal standing to acceptance.

---

# Canonical Gestures

## Strike

Removes standing.

Never removes visibility.

---

## Margin Note

Adds context.

Never replaces content.

---

## Circle

Draws attention.

Never creates importance.

---

## Underline

Records conviction.

Never decoration.

---

## Weight

Seals.

Never approves.

The founder alone places the weight.

---

## Lift

Completes the act.

Marks withdrawal.

Grants permanence.

---

# Human Choreography

Founder hesitates

→ The pencil rests on the paper.

Nothing fills the silence.

---

Stencil disagrees

→ A finger indicates.

The correction appears beside.

Never on top.

---

Founder discovers

→ The founder steps back.

The work changes scale.

Nothing new is added.

---

Founder commits

→ The sheet is squared.

The weight lands.

The surface changes status.

---

# Structural Invariants

Motion

Movement changes judgment.

Gesture

Touch changes authority.

Composition

Position changes understanding.

Spatial

Location changes standing.

The same act is described through different media.

---

# Design Consequences

Stop animating hands.

Start animating permission.

Stop drawing decorative marks.

Start recording consequential marks.

Stop replacing founder language.

Start annotating beside it.

Every gesture must answer:

"Who had the right to do this?"

---

# Counterexamples

- Scribbling over the founder's words
- AI rewriting in place
- Multi-purpose gestures
- Decorative underlines
- Animated flourish
- Automatic correction
- Forced completion

---

# Design Review

Ask:

Who performed this gesture?

Did they have jurisdiction?

Did the original remain visible?

Did withdrawal occur?

Can the gesture be refused?

Does the gesture preserve authorship?

If not,

the gesture is unconstitutional.

# Closing

Gesture Grammar is not about how a hand moves.

It is about whether that hand had the right to move.

Every touch alters responsibility.

Every mark carries jurisdiction.

Every withdrawal grants standing.

> **Gesture is jurisdiction: every touch declares the right to act upon something.**
