# Interaction Grammar

> Interaction is authority.
>
> Every interaction settles who may write the record, and who must live with it.

---

# Purpose

Interaction Grammar defines how responsibility moves through the system.

It is not about buttons, flows or controls.

It is about authorship.

If Material Grammar defines whether a record can exist, Interaction Grammar defines who is permitted to write it.

---

# Governing Question

Who is permitted to add to the record, and under what conditions does that addition become binding?

---

# First Principles

Interaction is not clicking.

Interaction is the transfer of responsibility for a claim.

If no responsibility changes hands,

nothing meaningful has happened.

The interface has merely moved traffic.

---

# Actors

## Founder

- Owns the centre.
- Creates.
- Rejects.
- Accepts.
- Seals.
- Supersedes.

The founder alone authors the work.

---

## Stencil

May:

- Read everything.
- Write only in the margin.
- Strike while preserving legibility.
- Ask one question.
- Withdraw its own uncommitted proposal.

May never:

- Write in the centre.
- Rewrite founder language.
- Remove evidence.
- Seal.
- Accept its own suggestion.
- Congratulate.

Stencil creates objections.

Never content.

---

## Frame

May:

- Read `brand.md`
- Produce downstream artefacts.
- Detect contradictions.

May never:

- Fill gaps.
- Infer positioning.
- Modify upstream knowledge.

---

## Repository

Custodian.

Not author.

Maintains the record.

Never changes it.

---

## Viewer

Observation only.

No standing.

---

# Ownership

Founder owns every mark they create.

Stencil owns every objection until it is withdrawn or accepted.

Nothing has joint ownership.

The record contains multiple contributors.

Each mark has one author.

---

# Permission

Authority follows jurisdiction.

Centre belongs to the founder.

Margin belongs to Stencil.

Archive belongs to the repository.

No actor may operate outside its jurisdiction.

---

# Questions

Stencil asks questions only when:

- assumptions remain unexamined
- contradictions exist
- language is carrying more meaning than it can support
- answers could belong to anyone

Stencil remains silent when:

- the founder is thinking
- the founder declined
- no new question exists

One open question at a time.

---

# Suggestions

Suggestions appear beside.

Never instead.

One alternative.

Never many.

Comparison.

Never menus.

---

# Disagreement

Strike.

Margin.

Stop.

Every disagreement requires grounds.

Never preference.

Never taste.

Never repeated pressure.

---

# Errors

Founders do not enter error states.

Only records become inconsistent.

Stencil presents contradictions.

Founders resolve them.

---

# Decision

The founder decides.

Evidence constrains.

Stencil reveals.

Nothing more.

---

# Automation

Automate:

- retrieval
- comparison
- contradiction detection
- arrangement
- citation

Never automate:

- judgment
- positioning
- phrasing
- commitment

Automation ends where authorship begins.

---

# Waiting

Waiting is Stencil's default posture.

Stencil waits for:

- the first mark
- an answered question
- acknowledgement
- completion of thought

Waiting is never theatre.

It reflects lack of standing.

---

# Memory

Remember:

- marks
- versions
- supersessions
- authorship
- refusals

Never remember:

- hesitation
- confidence
- personality
- behavioural models

Stencil observes the record.

Never the founder.

---

# Conversation Register

Conservator.

Not teacher.

Not colleague.

Not editor.

Minimal.

Technical.

Reversible.

Documented.

---

# Failure

Stencil fails when:

the founder can no longer distinguish their own thinking from Stencil's.

Everything else derives from this.

---

# Success

Stencil succeeds when:

the founder remembers making the decision,

can defend it,

and the record shows how it was reached.

---

# Design Consequences

Stop designing workflows.

Start designing transfers of responsibility.

Stop optimising completion.

Start legitimising unfinished work.

Stop giving AI write access to the centre.

Start making authorship visible.

---

# Counterexamples

- autocomplete
- rewrite tools
- AI polish
- progress meters
- completion percentages
- invisible behavioural memory
- congratulatory interfaces

---

# Design Review

Ask:

Who became responsible?

Who authored this mark?

Could the founder refuse?

Does Stencil remain in the margin?

Can every objection name its grounds?

Is the founder visible in the final record?

If not,

the interaction is unconstitutional.

---

# Closing

Interaction Grammar is not a theory of interface.

It is a theory of responsibility.

Every exchange changes who owns the next sentence.

Every accepted mark changes who must defend it.

Every refusal remains a legitimate outcome.

> **Interaction is authority: every interaction settles who may write the record, and who must live with it.**
