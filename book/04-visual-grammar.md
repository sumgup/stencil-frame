# Visual Grammar

> The visual is testimony.

> Every mark is a statement about what happened to this surface.

---

# Purpose

Visual Grammar defines how a surface makes its own history visible.

If Motion Grammar governs the truthfulness of time, Gesture Grammar governs the truthfulness of contact, Composition Grammar governs the truthfulness of arrangement, and Spatial Grammar governs the truthfulness of location, Visual Grammar governs the truthfulness of appearance.

It asks one question:

> **What makes something look like it was arrived at rather than produced?**

Visual design therefore exists to make derivation legible.

Beauty is permitted.

Opacity is not.

---

# Governing Question

What makes a surface appear honestly worked rather than merely styled?

---

# First Principles

Visual design is not making things beautiful.

It is making derivation legible.

Every mark should carry evidence of the decision that produced it.

A visual becomes dishonest the moment it claims work that never happened.

Appearance is therefore never autonomous.

It is always accountable to process.

---

# The Primary Mechanism

Everything in Stencil derives from one visual act:

## Correction

Correction is not one element among many.

Correction generates the entire visual language.

Every correction creates:

- Trace
- Evidence
- Contrast
- Imperfection

Correction therefore becomes the visual engine of the system.

---

# The Four Properties

## Trace

Trace is the visible path of making.

Pressure.

Drag.

Construction.

Registration.

Guide lines.

A trace proves that the work travelled.

---

## Evidence

Evidence is retained decision.

Rejected attempts remain visible.

Construction survives completion.

Versions coexist.

Nothing claims certainty by deleting uncertainty.

---

## Contrast

Contrast exists to separate intervention from substrate.

Near-monochrome surfaces.

One deliberate accent.

Difference is applied sparingly so intervention remains meaningful.

---

## Imperfection

Imperfection is residue.

Not style.

Every deviation must have a cause.

If nobody can explain why a mark differs, it should be removed.

---

Stencil speaks through artefacts rather than decoration.

Its native vocabulary includes:

- Strike-through

- Guide lines
- Margin notes

- Version stamps

- Graph paper
- Typewriter text
- Hand annotations
- Eraser ghosts
- Pin holes
- Fold lines
- Tape
- Photocopy degradation
- Scan edges

These are not stylistic motifs.

They are records of work.

---

# Colour

Colour never communicates importance.

Colour communicates intervention.

Near-monochrome remains the substrate.

Accent colour marks human judgment.

Blueprint blue may indicate construction.

Coral remains reserved for error.

Colour is rationed.

Meaning increases through scarcity.

---

# Texture

Texture is permitted only as residue.

Legal:

- Graphite tooth
- Pressure variation
- Scan artefacts
- Registration offset
- Photocopy degradation

Illegal:

- Vintage filters
- Artificial grain
- Coffee stains
- Paper textures
- Lens flare
- Digital noise
- Decorative roughness

Texture must originate from a process the document genuinely underwent.

---

# Illustration

Stencil illustrations are conceptual proofs rather than pictures.

Construction survives.

Guide lines remain.

Corrections remain.

Every illustration traces back to a named positioning decision.

If the trace column is empty, the illustration is decoration.

---

# Photography

Photography is documentation.

Never atmosphere.

Allowed:

- Overhead records
- Flatbed scans
- Proof sheets
- Real desks
- Real working artefacts

Forbidden:

- Stock photography
- Lifestyle imagery
- Hero portraits
- Mood photography
- Dramatic lighting
- Product glamour

The camera functions as a scanner.

Never an author.

---

# Design Consequences

Stop styling process.

Leave process behind.

Stop applying character.

Produce genuine residue.

Stop creating roughness.

Produce work that became rough.

Stop adding texture.

Capture texture created by work.

Appearance follows history.

Never precedes it.

---

# Design Review

Ask:

Could this have been produced in one pass?

Can every mark name the decision that caused it?

Did anything survive rejection?

Does the accent indicate intervention rather than importance?

Does texture come from a real process?

Could this visual exist without its history?

If the answer is no,

the appearance has become decorative.

# Closing

Visual Grammar is not a style.

It is a theory of honest appearance.

Nothing inside Stencil may look as though it travelled a road it never walked.

Every surface becomes testimony.

Every mark becomes evidence.

Every deviation becomes accountable.

> **The visual is testimony: every mark is a statement about what happened to this surface, and a mark that cannot name what happened is perjury.**
