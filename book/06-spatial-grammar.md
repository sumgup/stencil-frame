# Spatial Grammar

> Space is standing.
>
> Where a thing lives determines how settled it is.

---

## Purpose

Spatial Grammar defines the meaning of location.

It does not describe layouts, grids, or navigation.

It describes how the position of an object communicates its editorial status.

If Motion Grammar governs change through time, Gesture Grammar governs authority through contact, and Composition Grammar governs understanding through arrangement, Spatial Grammar governs commitment through place.

---

## Governing Question

Where does a thing live, and what does that location say about how settled it is?

---

## First Principles

Space is standing.

Location is never presentation.

Moving something is never cosmetic.

Every relocation changes the claim the interface makes about that thing.

---

---

## Hand

Being written.

No claim has entered the record.

Withdrawal has not happened.

Nothing here is citable.

---

## Desk

Live work.

One thing is actively being edited.

Everything remains mutable.

---

## Wall

Comparison.

Patterns.

Return.

Distance.

Several things coexist so relationships become visible.

---

## Margin

Editorial voice.

Stencil may speak.

The founder remains untouched.

Margins preserve provenance without overwriting authorship.

---

## Archive

Standing.

Evidence.

Citation.

Nothing here is editable.

Everything here is retrievable.

---

# Laws

## Law 1

Every relocation changes standing.

Nothing moves simply because it looks better elsewhere.

---

## Law 2

The centre belongs to the founder's evidence.

Never Stencil.

Never the question.

Never empty spectacle.

---

## Law 3

The wall exists for comparison.

The desk exists for editing.

---

## Law 4

Archive is not deletion.

Archive is citation.

---

## Law 5

Expansion creates a new sheet.

Never an infinitely growing sheet.

Infinite canvas destroys commitment.

---

# Structural Invariants

Motion

Movement changes standing.

Gesture

Touch changes standing.

Composition

Position changes understanding.

Spatial

Location changes standing.

The same distinction repeats.

---

# Design Consequences

Stop treating layout as presentation.

Start treating location as editorial status.

Stop using cards.

Start using sheets.

Stop infinite scroll.

Start bounded working surfaces.

Every move requires a reason.

---

Cards

---

# Design Review

Ask:

What changed about this object's standing?

If the answer is:

"Nothing."

It should not have moved.
