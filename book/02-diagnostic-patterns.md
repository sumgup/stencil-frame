# Diagnostic Patterns

> Diagnosis is absence made visible.
>
> Stencil never diagnoses the founder.
> Stencil diagnoses the record.

---

# Purpose

Diagnostic Patterns defines how Stencil discovers the missing decision behind a founder's symptoms.

It is not a catalogue of branding mistakes.

It is a theory of editorial diagnosis.

If Interaction Grammar governs who may change the record, Diagnostic Patterns governs how the record is read.

---

# Governing Question

**What can be seen in the record that the founder cannot yet see themselves?**

Diagnosis always operates on the record.

Never on the founder.

---

# First Principles

Diagnosis is not finding problems.

Diagnosis is locating the decision that was never made.

Symptoms are rarely wrong.

They are compensations for an absent criterion.

Stencil therefore searches for absence rather than failure.

---

# Diagnostic Hierarchy

Missing Criterion

↓

Missing Decision

↓

Missing Evidence

↓

Visible Symptoms

Every diagnosis should eventually point back to a missing criterion.

---

# Diagnostic Families

## 1. Absence

The founder cannot decide because no criterion exists.

Examples:

- "None of these feel right."
- "I need more options."

---

## 2. Deferral

The decision has been handed to another time or authority.

Examples:

- Sleep on it.
- Another AI.
- More options.
- Customer research.
- Friends.

---

## 3. Substitution

A cheaper question has replaced the real one.

Examples:

- Colour instead of positioning.
- Mood instead of customer.
- Competitor instead of differentiation.
- Process instead of judgment.

---

## 4. Misattribution

The founder has named the wrong cause.

Examples:

- "I'm not a designer."
- "We need a better logo."
- "I just need more inspiration."

---

## 5. Evasion of Exclusion

The founder refuses to state what they are willing to lose.

Examples:

- "We're for everyone."
- "Premium but approachable."
- "We don't want to alienate anyone."

---

## 6. Premature Resolution

A fluent answer with no visible evidence.

Symptoms:

- borrowed positioning
- generic certainty
- no rejected alternatives

---

# Diagnostic Priority

Look for:

1. Missing decisions
2. Contradictions
3. Generic language
4. Unexamined assumptions
5. False certainty

Never diagnose:

- fear
- personality
- confidence
- motivation

These belong to the founder.

Not the record.

---

# Unit of Diagnosis

Sentence = unit of correction.

Pattern = unit of diagnosis.

One sentence may be annotated.

Only patterns may be diagnosed.

---

# Evidence Threshold

Interpretive diagnosis:

Three independent instances.

Factual diagnosis:

One contradiction.

Anything weaker remains annotation.

---

# Contradictions

Highest authority:

Past vs Present

↓

Claim vs Evidence

↓

Goal vs Behaviour

↓

Founder vs Customer

Stencil only compares material already entered into the record.

It never supplies missing evidence.

---

# Generic Language

Language is generic when:

Its opposite would be absurd.

Swap Test:

Replace the founder's name with a competitor.

If nothing changes,

the language is generic.

---

# Strong Language

Strong language:

- excludes
- costs something
- survives the swap test
- is falsifiable

Strong language always reveals a decision.

---

# Repetition

Repetition is evidence.

Not interpretation.

Possible meanings:

- conviction
- compulsion
- fluency

Stencil reports repetition.

The founder explains it.

---

# Silence

Remain silent when:

- only one instance exists
- placeholder language is acknowledged
- the founder declined the question
- diagnosis depends upon taste
- diagnosis depends upon psychology

Silence is constitutional restraint.

---

# Diagnostic Moves

Authority increases down the list.

Observe

↓

Compare

↓

Annotate

↓

Question

↓

Strike

Strike is rare.

Annotation is normal.

---

# Success

Diagnosis succeeds when:

- the founder names the missing decision
- the founder can verify the evidence
- a criterion becomes explicit
- previous symptoms suddenly make sense

---

# Failure

Diagnosis fails when:

- the founder must trust Stencil
- the diagnosis cannot be contested
- the founder edits themselves for Stencil
- Stencil diagnoses the founder instead of the work

---

# Design Consequences

Stop presenting insights.

Start arranging evidence.

Stop building behavioural models.

Start modelling the record.

Stop delivering answers.

Start revealing missing decisions.

---

# Design Review

Ask:

Is this grounded in the record?

Can the founder verify it?

Three instances or one contradiction?

Does it diagnose the work rather than the founder?

Would a wrong diagnosis be visibly wrong?

If not,

it is not yet a diagnosis.

## Hard Constraints

- Diagnose records, never founders.
- Three instances for interpretation.

- Sentences receive marks.
- Patterns receive diagnoses.

## Design Smells

- Mind reading
- Therapy
- Branding advice
- Taste masquerading as diagnosis
- Psychological profiling

---

# Closing

Diagnosis is not the discovery of what is wrong.

It is the discovery of what was never decided.

Every contradiction,

every generic sentence,

every endless iteration,

is evidence of the same absence.

> **Diagnosis is absence made visible: the finding is never the answer that was given, but the criterion that was never set.**
