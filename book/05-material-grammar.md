# Material Grammar

> Material is disposition.
>
> A substance is defined by what it refuses to give up.

---

# Purpose

Material Grammar defines which substances are capable of carrying evidence.

It is not a catalogue of textures or physical objects.

It is a theory of retention.

If Visual Grammar governs whether history is told honestly, Material Grammar governs whether history can exist at all.

---

# Governing Question

**What substances can hold a mark long enough for it to become evidence?**

Everything in this grammar derives from one property:

**Retention under revision.**

Can the material receive a mark, preserve it, and receive another mark without destroying the first?

---

# First Principles

Material is not what something is made from.

It is what a thing is willing to keep.

Materials are defined by their behaviour toward marks.

They either:

- retain
- release
- overwrite
- obscure

These are moral behaviours inside Stencil rather than sensory qualities.

A material that erases cleanly is not neutral.

It destroys the record.

---

# Native Materials

## Carrier Materials

These carry marks and preserve history.

- Paper
- Pencil
- Tracing paper
- Graph paper
- Index cards
- Photocopies
- Scanned documents

---

## Apparatus Materials

These organize evidence rather than carrying it.

- Desk
- Wall
- Folder
- Paper clip
- Binder clip
- Pin
- Tape (only with cause)
- Scanner
- Printer

---

## Conditional Materials

Allowed only when their behaviour supports the grammar.

### Blueprint

Construction register.

Never decoration.

---

### Ink

Only for sealing.

Never for working.

---

### Tape

Repair.

Attachment.

Never styling.

---

# Foreign Materials

The following contradict the grammar.

- Whiteboards
- Markers
- Sticky notes
- Correction fluid
- Highlighters
- Glass
- Chrome
- Decorative plastic
- Floating cards
- Device mockups
- Artificial paper textures

They either erase history,

simulate history,

or refuse to retain it.

---

# Pencil

Pencil is the native working material.

It is:

- reversible
- pressure sensitive
- annotatable
- imperfect through use

Erasing leaves residue.

Residue becomes evidence.

---

# Paper

Paper is native because it has an edge.

An edge makes commitment possible.

Paper is finite.

When full,

a new sheet must begin.

This naturally creates:

- supersession
- archive
- provenance

Paper is not valued because it is analogue.

It is valued because it can carry history.

---

# Tracing Paper

Tracing paper is the material of supersession.

It allows:

old version

+

new version

to remain visible simultaneously.

It is therefore the physical form of Completion.

---

# Photocopy

A photocopy proves an original existed.

Generation loss is accepted because it is caused.

Nothing is simulated.

Everything records an event.

---

# Material Hierarchy

Scanned

↓

Drawn

↓

Typed

↓

Written

↓

Printed

Scanning becomes the preferred finish because it preserves every previous layer while adding its own generation history.

---

# Native Objects

Examples include:

- pencil
- eraser
- ruler
- triangle
- T-square
- compass
- binder clip
- paper weight
- manila folder
- graph paper
- tracing paper
- flatbed scanner
- pinboard
- drafting table

Objects belong because of what they enable,

not because of how they look.

---

# Digital Materials

Digital materials are judged by the same standard.

Native:

- Version history
- Diff
- Plain text
- Cursor
- Selection
- Audit trail
- Monospace

Foreign:

- Autosave without versions
- Invisible undo
- Skeleton loaders
- Toast notifications
- Decorative hover states

Digital materials are accepted when they preserve evidence rather than replacing it.

---

# Aging

Material does not age.

Material records events.

Wear is acceptable.

Weather is not.

Generational loss is acceptable.

Artificial aging is not.

---

# Damage

Damage is evidence only when it can name the event that caused it.

Examples:

- clip marks
- fold lines
- eraser ghosts
- pencil indentation

Forbidden:

- coffee stains
- vintage filters
- artificial tears
- distressed textures

Every deviation must have an author.

---

# Weight

Only one material carries true weight.

The paper weight.

Everything else remains intentionally light.

Mass is reserved for commitment.

---

# Sound

Sound belongs only to status changes.

Working remains quiet.

Correction may scratch.

Paper may settle.

The weight may land.

Everything else remains silent.

---

# Design Consequences

Stop choosing materials because they look authentic.

Start choosing materials because they preserve history.

Stop simulating physical materials.

Start identifying digital materials with equivalent behaviour.

Behaviour outranks appearance.

---

# Counterexamples

- Whiteboard thinking
- Infinite canvas
- Decorative paper textures
- Vintage filters
- Artificial grain
- Rich visual skins without provenance
- Materials that overwrite history

---

# Design Review

Ask:

Can this material preserve evidence?

Does it retain previous marks?

Can it be sealed?

Can every change be traced?

Does degradation have a cause?

Would history survive if this material were removed?

If not,

it is the wrong material.

---

# Closing

Material Grammar is not about paper.

It is about memory.

A material belongs in Stencil only if it can receive history,

preserve history,

and surrender it without pretending it never happened.

> **Material is disposition: a substance is defined by what it refuses to give up.**
