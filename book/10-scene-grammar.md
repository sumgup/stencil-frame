# Scene Grammar

> A scene is deposition.
>
> The camera testifies to what it found, and may be cross-examined.

---

# Purpose

Scene Grammar defines how Stencil records moments of judgment.

It is not a cinematography guide.

It is a theory of evidence.

Every previous grammar governs the record itself.

Scene Grammar governs the making of a record of that record.

The camera is never an author.

It is a witness.

---

# Governing Question

**How does a moment of judgment become visible without the camera claiming authorship?**

---

# First Principles

A scene is not a depiction of work.

A scene is a record of a decision captured by something that had no hand in making it.

Nothing may be staged for the camera.

Nothing may exist in frame because it looks better there.

Every object must be able to explain why it is present.

---

# Protagonist

The protagonist is never the founder.

Never Stencil.

Never the paper.

The protagonist is **the decision**.

Paper is merely the vehicle through which that decision becomes visible.

The frame follows the decision rather than the person.

If the founder leaves and the work remains,

the camera stays with the work.

---

# Camera

The camera is a witness.

It records.

It never persuades.

It never participates.

It never performs the realization for the viewer.

Its responsibility is honesty rather than interpretation.

---

# Camera Position

Two positions define the entire language.

## Overhead

Perpendicular to the desk.

The copy-stand view.

The scanner's perspective.

The default.

---

## Standing

Eye level.

Facing the wall.

Used only when relationships between sheets become the subject.

---

Forbidden:

- POV
- Dutch angles
- Hero angles
- Diminishing angles

---

# Distance

Move closer only when contact occurs.

Move back only when relationships become visible.

Never move to create excitement.

Distance follows judgment.

Never pacing.

---

# Lens

Default:

Normal lens.

Macro:

Only to reveal physical history.

Wide:

Only for documentary overheads.

Telephoto:

Only for comparison.

Portrait optics are forbidden.

---

# Framing

The frame is precise.

Its contents remain contingent.

Nothing inside the frame is arranged for beauty.

Everything sits where work left it.

---

# Light

Light exists only to make evidence checkable.

Flat.

Even.

Neutral.

The only permitted dramatic light reveals physical traces such as graphite, indentation or erased marks.

Light never creates hierarchy.

---

# Shadow

Shadow may exist.

Shadow may never conceal.

Every important mark remains readable.

No artificial shadows.

No digital shadows.

---

# Focus

Deep focus is the default.

Everything remains inspectable.

Shallow depth of field is permitted only where physical optics make it unavoidable, such as macro photography.

---

# Camera Movement

The camera remains locked.

Movement belongs to the subject.

Not the witness.

Changes in viewpoint occur through cuts.

Never through camera movement.

---

# Human Presence

Hands are primary.

Faces are rare.

The founder appears through action rather than emotion.

Gesture carries meaning.

Reaction does not.

---

# Objects

Objects appear because they were used.

Never because they improve composition.

Priority:

Paper

↓

Pencil

↓

Desk

↓

Wall

↓

Apparatus

↓

Everything else

---

# Empty Space

Space remains available.

Capacity matters.

The desk is always larger than the current work.

The wall always has room for another sheet.

Emptiness is possibility.

Not decoration.

---

# Colour

Neutral.

Near monochrome.

Any accent must come from an actual intervention rather than styling.

---

# Sound

Primary sounds:

- paper
- pencil
- room tone
- silence
- the paper weight

Music is absent except at true completion.

---

# Editing

Hard cuts.

Long takes.

Cuts occur on stillness.

Never on action.

Never dissolve.

Never soften joins.

The join is evidence.

---

# Illustration

Illustration replaces photography only when relationships rather than objects become the subject.

Construction remains visible.

---

# Animation

Animation is reserved for state changes.

Never atmosphere.

Never decoration.

Every animation must correspond to an actual change of standing.

---

# UI

UI exists inside the workspace.

Never floating.

Never isolated.

It behaves like another sheet on the desk.

---

# Hero Scene

Overhead.

A contact sheet.

Thirty-six frames.

Thirty-five crossed out.

One circled.

Frame twelve missing.

No explanation.

The absence carries the argument.

The second look completes the scene.

---

# Success

A scene succeeds when:

- the viewer can reconstruct the decision
- every object can justify its presence
- the camera has not directed interpretation
- understanding arrives through inspection
- the second look reveals more than the first

---

# Failure

A scene fails when:

- style arrives before evidence
- emotion replaces inspection
- beauty replaces derivation
- props become decoration
- the viewer feels before they understand

---

# Design Consequences

Stop styling scenes.

Start recording work.

Stop directing attention.

Start preserving evidence.

Stop filming products.

Start filming decisions.

---

# Design Review

Ask:

Can every object explain why it is here?

Could this have happened without the camera?

Is the witness honest?

Does the second look matter?

Has the camera remained outside the decision?

If not,

the scene is not yet constitutional.

## Hard Constraints

- Camera never authors.
- Movement belongs to subjects.
- Every object requires a cause.
- Deep focus by default.
- Hard cuts only.
- Hero follows the decision.

## Design Smells

- Product glamour
- Lifestyle photography
- Camera movement
- Emotional manipulation
- Styled workspaces
- Decorative props

---

# Closing

Scene Grammar is not about how Stencil looks.

It is about how Stencil can be believed.

Every frame is testimony.

Every object is evidence.

Every cut is a declared join.

Every scene becomes a record that the viewer can inspect rather than trust.

> **A scene is deposition: the camera testifies to what it found, and may be cross-examined.**
